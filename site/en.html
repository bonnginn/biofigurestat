From input to analysis and graphs

For example, before comparing Control with Treatment A and B, confirm whether each value comes from an independent unit or from repeated measurements of the same unit. Group names and numerical values alone do not define the experimental structure.

  1. Enter the experiment and data

    Start a New Experiment and confirm treatments and grouping, measurements, experimental units or samples, and relationships between conditions. Enter data in the generated table, import an Excel workbook or paste CSV / TSV data. You can also start by graphing an existing table.

  2. Confirm the structure and run the analyses you need

    In Statistics, review the experimental-unit n, pairing, comparison scope and cautions. Supported structures include independent, paired, multi-group and repeated-measure comparisons, correlation and simple regression, proportions and contingency tables, and Kaplan–Meier / log-rank workflows. Unsupported or ambiguous structures stop rather than being replaced with a nearby method.

  3. Adjust and save the graph

    Adjust points, summaries, error bars, series, axes and statistical annotations, and export SVG / PNG / CSV. Appearance editing does not change analysis results. Save a local .lsa project that retains the input, experimental structure, analysis provenance and Graph, then reopen it to check the result.

Detailed quick start →

Make biological n, pairing and repeated identity explicit

  • Experimental unit and biological n: Confirm the unit or sample that received the treatment. Counts of cells, ROIs, wells or table rows are not automatically independent n.
  • Pairing: Confirm IDs for the same unit or sample, or matching defined by the experiment. Sharing a row between independent groups, or sharing an experimental date, does not establish pairing.
  • Repeated measurements and nesting: Distinguish measurements of the same unit at different times or conditions from measurements nested within a unit. Ordinary ANOVA or another nearby method is not substituted for an unsupported structure.

Analysis guidance is based on the declared experimental structure and comparison goal. Scientific and statistical review by the researcher remains necessary.

Macs require Apple Silicon arm64 and macOS 11 or later. Windows builds are unsigned; the Mac build is ad-hoc signed and not Apple-notarized. The operating system may show a security warning.

Official release notes, SHA-256 digests and validation limits should be reviewed before use. On Mac, extract the ZIP and open BioFigureStat.app. See also the Mac verification and opening instructions.

Alpha limitations and data handling

  • Keep research data and .lsa backed up separately. Reopen saved projects and inspect Graphs, statistical results, Methods and exports.
  • Advanced mixed-effects models, Cox / competing-risks survival, arbitrary complex factorial structures, nested X/Y inference, and broad dose-response / global-fit workflows are outside Alpha. Heatmaps are visualization-only.
  • Standard statistics run deterministically and locally. Research data and files are not uploaded automatically. Usage reporting requires explicit opt-in; each problem report requires content review and an explicit send action.
  • AI Help is not the statistical engine. External-LLM consultation is a manual copy-and-paste action by the user.
