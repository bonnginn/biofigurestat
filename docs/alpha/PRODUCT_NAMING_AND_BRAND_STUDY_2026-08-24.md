# Product naming and exploratory brand study

Date: 2026-08-24  
Status: decision support only; no product or repository rename is authorized

## Scope and screening method

The development name `Life Science Analysis App` describes the category but is not distinctive.
The naming target is a calm, memorable identity for a local-first application that starts from
experiment structure, preserves the experimental unit, performs deterministic statistics, creates
research graphs, and retains methods/provenance.

This study applied four filters:

1. not limited to a single assay or statistical test;
2. pronounceable in English and reasonably transliterable into Japanese;
3. not intentionally similar to GraphPad Prism or another established analysis product;
4. no obvious current collision in a basic exact-name web/product search.

This is **not legal clearance**. WIPO says its Global Brand Database spans international and
participating national/regional collections, but recommends searching national/regional registers
as well. USPTO likewise recommends searching similar marks and assessing likelihood of confusion.
Before adoption, a human-led clearance should cover exact, phonetic, and visually similar marks in
the intended jurisdictions and goods/services classes.

- WIPO Global Brand Database: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/global-brand-database
- WIPO availability guidance: https://www.wipo.int/en/web/madrid-system/check-availability
- USPTO trademark search guidance: https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search
- EUIPO search tools: https://www.euipo.europa.eu/search

The competitive naming space is already crowded with short statistical and graph terms. GraphPad
Prism links data, analyses, and graphs; Origin describes itself as data-analysis and graphing
software; JASP and jamovi both occupy the short, friendly, open-statistics naming space. A future
name should therefore emphasize this product's experiment-structure and provenance advantage,
rather than another synonym for `stats` or `plot`.

- GraphPad Prism statistics guide: https://www.graphpad.com/guides/prism/latest/statistics/stat_statistical_analyses_with_pris.htm
- Origin/OriginPro: https://www.originlab.com/Origin
- JASP: https://jasp-stats.org/
- jamovi: https://www.jamovi.org/

## Shortlist: seven names

### 1. AssayArc

- **Rationale:** conveys the whole journey from experiment definition to defensible output rather
  than statistics alone.
- **Pronunciation:** `ASS-ay ark`; Japanese: アッセイアーク.
- **Impression:** scientific, structured, purposeful; moderately warm rather than clinical.
- **International usability:** familiar English components; easy to say, though `assay` may be less
  familiar outside experimental science.
- **Likely abbreviation:** AA is too generic; the full name should normally be used.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** exact phrase appeared relatively quiet, but `ARC` is heavily used in
  science and software.
- **Obvious risk:** an adjacent scientific product already uses ARC as a product name; legal and
  confusion review is essential despite the distinct compound.

### 2. AssayWeave

- **Rationale:** expresses the product's integration of design, replicate identity, data, analysis,
  graph, and provenance.
- **Pronunciation:** `ASS-ay weev`; Japanese: アッセイウィーヴ.
- **Impression:** modern, data-centered, collaborative without implying cloud collaboration.
- **International usability:** meaningful in English; `weave` pronunciation/spelling may require a
  short learning moment in some languages.
- **Likely abbreviation:** AW, although the full name is preferable.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** no obvious exact-name product surfaced in the basic screen.
- **Obvious risk:** slightly longer; “assay” can sound narrower than the full range of experiments.

### 3. Unitara

- **Rationale:** foregrounds the product's most distinctive scientific principle—the correct
  experimental unit—without limiting it to graphing or statistics.
- **Pronunciation:** `you-ni-TAH-ra` or `YOU-ni-tara`; Japanese: ユニタラ. One pronunciation must be
  standardized.
- **Impression:** calm, capable, product-like, and extensible.
- **International usability:** short and easy to transliterate; meaning is less self-evident than the
  two Assay names.
- **Likely abbreviation:** UNA or UTA, neither recommended until collision screening.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** Unitara Motors is active; `unitāra` is also a Latvian-language word.
- **Obvious risk:** exact company-name collision outside software and uncertain domain position.

### 4. AssayFrame

- **Rationale:** the experimental design “frames” valid analysis and presentation.
- **Pronunciation:** `ASS-ay frame`; Japanese: アッセイフレーム.
- **Impression:** rigorous and understandable, with a product/system feel.
- **International usability:** straightforward English components.
- **Likely abbreviation:** AF is too generic.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** `assayFrame` appears as a technical class/object in an R package.
- **Obvious risk:** weak search distinctiveness in bioassay documentation.

### 5. DatumWeave

- **Rationale:** emphasizes structured observations and provenance rather than a test menu.
- **Pronunciation:** `DAY-tum weev`; Japanese: データムウィーヴ.
- **Impression:** technical, modern, precise.
- **International usability:** “datum” is recognizable but may feel formal or be pronounced
  inconsistently.
- **Likely abbreviation:** DW, too generic for a public-facing shorthand.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** no obvious exact-name product surfaced.
- **Obvious risk:** weaker immediate life-science signal.

### 6. Replicove

- **Rationale:** suggests a safe, local home for biological replicates and their linked outputs.
- **Pronunciation:** recommended `REP-li-cove`; Japanese: レプリコーヴ.
- **Impression:** calm and protective; less corporate.
- **International usability:** coined and relatively easy to spell once heard.
- **Likely abbreviation:** RC, too generic.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** no obvious exact-name product surfaced.
- **Obvious risk:** users may hear “replica,” “replicate,” or “repli-cove” differently; it needs spoken
  name testing.

### 7. Reprovia

- **Rationale:** a reproducible route (`repro` + `via`) from experiment to methods and figure.
- **Pronunciation:** `ree-PRO-vee-ah`; Japanese: リプロヴィア.
- **Impression:** scientific and process-oriented.
- **International usability:** compact, but the intended word construction is not instantly clear.
- **Likely abbreviation:** RV, unsuitable because of many existing meanings.
- **Search/domain ambiguity:** unrelated printing-company use surfaced.
- **Obvious risk:** `repro` often means reproduction or reprographics rather than reproducibility.

Recommended next action: run a short blinded name test with life-science researchers. Show only the
name and one-sentence product description, randomize order, and ask (a) what they think the product
does, (b) how they would pronounce/spell it, (c) trustworthiness, and (d) memorability after one day.
Do not ask which logo they prefer until semantic fit has been tested.

## Exploratory brand and logo directions

These are visual territories, not finished marks. Every direction should first work as a one-color
16–24 px icon, then at toolbar, app tile, About page, and documentation sizes.

### Direction A — Linked experimental units

Best fit: **Unitara**, also usable for **AssayArc**.

- Icon: two or three small observation dots connected vertically or diagonally to a stable anchor;
  a restrained axis corner provides structure.
- Meaning: the same experimental unit remains identifiable across conditions/time while subordinate
  observations are not mistaken for biological `n`.
- Style: round caps, consistent line weight, no literal cells, microscope, or DNA.
- Small-size rule: at 16 px reduce to three dots, one link, and one baseline; do not retain tiny nested
  marks.
- Light/dark: use a single foreground color plus optional muted accent; geometry must survive pure
  black/white.
- Risk: connected-dot marks are common, so the spacing/negative space must be distinctive and a logo
  similarity search is necessary.

### Direction B — Observations become a summary

Best fit: **AssayWeave**.

- Icon: three fine data strands/rows enter separately and resolve into one clean summary point or
  horizontal estimate line.
- Meaning: raw observations, unit summaries, statistics, graph, and provenance remain linked without
  being collapsed incorrectly.
- Style: calm geometric paths, not a gradient ribbon and not an AI “spark.”
- Small-size rule: use at most three incoming lines and one output mark; avoid crossings that fill in
  at favicon size.
- Light/dark: monochrome core with one optional desaturated teal/indigo accent.
- Risk: weaving can look textile-oriented; pair early sketches with the wordmark and test category
  recognition.

### Direction C — Structured experiment arc

Best fit: **AssayArc**.

- Icon: a low arc built from three modular nodes over a short baseline/axis; the final node can also
  read as a plotted data point.
- Meaning: design → data → analysis/figure, with no step hidden or detached from provenance.
- Style: stable and architectural, avoiding rainbow/aurora treatments and Prism-like facets.
- Small-size rule: three nodes and one arc only; no embedded letters at 16 px.
- Light/dark: flat one-color silhouette first; optional accent only on the final output node.
- Risk: arcs and node paths are widely used. The combination must be screened visually against
  scientific software, graph databases, and workflow tools.

## Human decisions required

No code or packaging identity should change until these decisions are made:

1. **Semantic center:** should the brand lead with the whole experimental journey (`AssayArc`), linked
   scientific evidence (`AssayWeave`), or experimental-unit correctness (`Unitara`)?
2. **Audience breadth:** confirm whether `assay` feels natural to animal, physiology, imaging, and
   computational researchers as well as wet-lab assay users.
3. **Name testing:** select two or three candidates for spoken and written testing in Japanese and
   English; settle the pronunciation of Unitara if it remains.
4. **Legal clearance scope:** choose initial markets and obtain professional exact/phonetic/similarity
   searches, likely including downloadable software and software-service classes. This report is not
   a legal opinion.
5. **Digital identity:** decide whether a `.com` is required or whether an organization/project domain
   is acceptable; check domains, package registries, GitHub organizations, and major social handles
   at the decision date.
6. **Visual territory:** choose linked units, observation-to-summary, or structured arc before any
   polished logo is commissioned.
7. **Final mark:** perform a visual trademark/similarity screen and accessibility checks before
   choosing the app icon, wordmark, or colors.

## Recommendation

Advance **AssayArc**, **AssayWeave**, and **Unitara** to human testing, but treat none as cleared or
selected. Keep the current development name in code and artifacts until both semantic testing and
professional clearance are complete. If adjacent `ARC` usage makes AssayArc unacceptable, promote
**AssayFrame** as the next descriptive candidate; if a more distinctive coined name is preferred,
continue from **Unitara** rather than weakening into generic `Graph`, `Plot`, or `Stat` compounds.
